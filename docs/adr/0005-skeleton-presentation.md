# 0005 — Skeletonised dial-side presentation

The calibre is presented skeletonised from the dial side: the main plate and bridges are cut away, the motion works are visible beneath the hands, and the balance sits at six o'clock where the viewer can watch it beat. The case is a thin polished band with a fluted crown at three o'clock and a sapphire chapter ring instead of an opaque dial.

**Why:** a faithful watch hides the escapement under the back and the motion works under the dial. Since the product's job is explanation, hiding the two most instructive assemblies would be self-defeating. The skeleton is a genuine watchmaking genre, so this is an art-directed presentation rather than a physical impossibility, and the README labels it as such. Keeping the chapter ring preserves the ability to actually read the time.

**Consequences:** the composition must work without a dial to hide behind, so bridge silhouettes and layer depth carry the design. The exploded view separates dial-side presentation layers (chapter ring, hands, motion works) from the movement proper, which makes the illusion legible rather than confusing.
